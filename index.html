AUSTRALIA'S FOREST LANDSCAPE

Australia's Changing Forests

Australia is often imagined as a vast dry continent, yet more than 133 million hectares are classified as forest. Those forests are not evenly distributed and the landscape we see today is not the landscape Australia always had.

What forests remain today, where are they found, and how has Australia's vegetation changed?

THE BIG PICTURE

Forest is a small part of Australia's land, but almost all of it is native.

The Forests of Australia 2023 dataset classifies approximately 133.6 million hectares as forest. That is around 17.4% of Australia's mapped land area. The first layer of the visualisation compares forest with non-forest land. The second layer opens that forest share to reveal what it contains.

THE MAIN FINDING

Native forest makes up about 98% of Australia's mapped forest area.

Australia's forest footprint is overwhelmingly native. Commercial plantations represent only a very small proportion of the national forest landscape.

Source: Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences, Forests of Australia 2023, National Forest Inventory.

02

WHERE THE FORESTS ARE

Australia's forests are concentrated in only a few parts of the country.

The national total hides a strong geographic pattern. Some states contain vast areas of mapped forest while others contribute only a small share. Comparing the map with the ranked state profile reveals where Australia's forest estate is concentrated.

FOREST DISTRIBUTION BY STATE

Where is Australia's forest concentrated?

The map colours each state and territory by its mapped forest area. The ranking beside it makes the differences easier to compare. Hover over a state to inspect its forest area and share of the national total.

Hover over the map or state ranking to explore the distribution.

01

START WITH THE MAP

Forest area varies substantially across the country. The strongest concentration occurs in Queensland, while several southern jurisdictions contain much smaller absolute forest areas.

02

COMPARE THE RANKING

Queensland contains approximately 52 million hectares of mapped forest. The Northern Territory, Western Australia and New South Wales form the next major group.

THE MAIN FINDING

Queensland alone contains almost two fifths of Australia's mapped forest area.

THE GEOGRAPHIC PATTERN

Australia's forest area is not evenly distributed. Queensland, the Northern Territory, Western Australia and New South Wales account for most of the mapped forest estate.

Source: Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences, Forests of Australia 2023, National Forest Inventory.

03

INSIDE THE FOREST

Australia's forest estate is dominated by a handful of eucalypt formations.

Forest area is not divided evenly between forest types. Some formations cover tens of millions of hectares, while others occupy only a small fraction of the national forest estate.

FOREST TYPE COMPOSITION

Which forest types dominate Australia?

Each radial segment represents one forest type. Its area is proportional to the amount of mapped forest it occupies. Larger rectangles therefore represent more widespread forest formations.

Hover over a segment to inspect its forest type, category, area and share of Australia's mapped forest.

01

LOOK AT THE LARGEST BLOCK

Eucalypt Medium Woodland is by far Australia's largest mapped forest type, covering about 54.4 million hectares.

02

EUCALYPTS DOMINATE

Several of the largest forest types are eucalypt formations, including medium open forest, mallee woodland and low woodland.

THE MAIN FINDING

Eucalypt Medium Woodland alone accounts for around 41% of Australia's mapped forest area.

WHAT THIS REVEALS

Australia's forests are diverse, but their area is strongly concentrated in a small number of dominant forest formations.

Source: Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences, Forests of Australia 2023, National Forest Inventory.

The same forest types do not dominate everywhere.

Australia's national forest profile hides strong regional differences. Comparing forest types across states and territories reveals which formations define different parts of the country.

THE STRUCTURE OF AUSTRALIA'S FORESTS

Most Australian forest is neither tall nor dense.

Forests differ not only by species, but also by their physical structure. Combining tree height with crown cover reveals the form of Australia's forest landscape, from low open woodland to tall closed forest.

Australia's forest landscape is dominated by relatively open woodland structures rather than tall, densely closed forest.
